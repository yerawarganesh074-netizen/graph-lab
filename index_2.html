Graph Lab

Four everyday systems, modelled as graphs you can touch. Drag the nodes, change the edges and watch the algorithms answer.

Vertices
Users of the platform.
Edges
A directed edge u → v means "u follows v". Two opposite edges make a friendship.
Questions it answers
Who is influential (in-degree), who can reach whom (BFS), which groups are closed (strongly connected components), who to suggest (walks of length 2).

Vertices
Three houses and three utilities: water, gas, electricity.
Edges
One pipe or cable from every house to every utility. This is the complete bipartite graph K3,3.
Question
Can all nine lines be drawn on a flat plane without any two crossing? In graph terms: is K3,3 planar?

Click a house, then a utility, to add or remove a line. Click a line to remove it. Drag any node to untangle.

Vertices
Web pages. Real web graphs have billions, so they are stored as adjacency lists.
Edges
A hyperlink from page p to page q is the directed edge p → q. A link is a vote for the page it points to.
Question
Which pages matter? PageRank scores a page by the scores of the pages linking to it, and the scores settle after repeated iteration.

Vertices
Places and intersections.
Edges
Road segments weighted by distance or travel time. A two-way road is a pair of directed edges; a one-way road is a single arrow.
Question
What is the cheapest route between two places? Dijkstra's algorithm settles the closest unsettled vertex one at a time.

Click a road to close it and watch the route change. The map is a schematic of Pune with rounded, illustrative distances and times, not real map data.
